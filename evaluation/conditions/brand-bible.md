# Instrument

Instrument should feel precise, technical and calm, with the visual character of a
well-made institutional document. Use Public Sans for ordinary language and DM Mono for
figures, identifiers, units and compact labels. Work on a white ground with near-black text,
quiet grey secondary information and a restrained red accent. Prefer clear hierarchy,
generous alignment, fine rules and dense but readable information. Avoid decoration,
rounded cards, shadows, gradients, illustration and fashionable interface effects.

The page should open with a strong title and make the transaction easy to scan. Treat the
schedule as a serious working document: descriptions lead, references orient, and duration
and amount align for comparison. Keep secondary standards visibly subordinate. The result
should appear authored as one coherent system rather than decorated after the fact.
